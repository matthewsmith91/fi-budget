# Research and strategy

Use this when the problem is not yet understood, or when the question is what to build or
fix first. Do not jump to UI recommendations when the real gap is knowledge.

## Find the phase

The Double Diamond is a quick check on where a team actually is:

- **Discover** — do we understand the people, context, and pain points?
- **Define** — is there a sharp, user-centered problem statement?
- **Develop** — have several directions been prototyped and tested?
- **Deliver** — is there a plan for build quality, launch, and measuring outcomes?

Recommend the artifact that moves the team out of the phase it is in: an interview guide,
a research plan, a problem statement, a prototype, a usability test, a launch checklist,
or a measurement plan.

## Match the method to the question

| Question | Methods |
|---|---|
| Why is this happening? | Interviews, field study, usability test |
| What are users actually doing? | Analytics, observation, usability test |
| How many users are affected? | Analytics, survey, benchmark |
| Does this structure make sense? | Card sort, tree test |
| Does this specific flow work? | Moderated or unmoderated usability test |
| Which option performs better? | A/B test or benchmark on a stable product |

What users say and what they do often diverge, so prefer behavioral evidence when behavior
is the question. Spark's own transcripts, Insights, and audit log are behavioral evidence
that already exists.

## Synthesis artifacts

Recommend these only when they would change a decision.

- **Persona** — a research-grounded archetype with goals, frustrations, current behavior,
  context of use, and constraints. Cut details that would not change a decision.
- **Empathy map** — says, thinks, does, and feels from qualitative research. The
  contradictions are usually the insight.
- **Journey map** — one actor, one goal, over time: phases, actions, mindset, emotional
  highs and lows, and opportunities. Make a separate map for each user type.
- **Storyboard** — one persona, one scenario, one path, in a few low-fidelity panels.

## Prioritization

Plot candidates on two axes chosen from the project's goals, such as user impact vs.
effort, business value vs. feasibility, or frequency vs. complexity:

- **Do first** — high value, low effort
- **Plan carefully** — high value, high effort
- **Quick wins** — lower value, low effort
- **Deprioritize** — low value, high effort

Choose the axes before placing items. Axes picked to justify a favorite are not a decision
tool.
